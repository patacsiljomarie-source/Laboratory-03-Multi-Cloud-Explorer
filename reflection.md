# Mission Reflection

Completing Mission 3 as a member of the Cloud Evaluation Team gave me a much deeper understanding of how the three major cloud providers compare in practice, not just in theory.

**1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?**
Google Cloud Platform impressed me the most, mainly because of its strength in AI/ML tools and its Kubernetes ecosystem, since Google originally created Kubernetes. Its clean console and competitive pricing also made it approachable for someone still learning cloud concepts.

**2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?**
Despite their differences, all three providers offer the same core categories of services: virtual machines, object storage, identity and access management, and managed SQL databases. They also all rely on the same fundamental structure of regions and availability zones to provide global reach and high availability.

**3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?**
Business requirements are the biggest factor in choosing a cloud provider. A startup with a limited budget benefits from flexible, low-cost pricing, while a university already using Microsoft tools benefits more from Azure's native integration. An AI research company needs strong GPU support, and a global e-commerce company needs a provider with the widest infrastructure footprint for high availability. There is no single "best" provider, only the one that fits the client's needs.

**4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?**
I learned that technical decisions in the cloud are rarely just about which platform has the best service. They are about matching a provider's strengths to a client's real business constraints, such as budget, existing infrastructure, and technical goals.

**5. How has your GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio improved after completing this mission?**
My portfolio now includes structured comparison documents, client recommendation reports, and hands-on Linux investigation notes, demonstrating both research skills and practical cloud reasoning I can showcase to future employers.
